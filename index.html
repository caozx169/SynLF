Overview

From RGB-D supervision to real light-field depth

Monocular depth is scale-ambiguous. Light-field cameras provide metric cues through single-lens micro-baselines, but learned methods lack real-world supervision and often fail on weak texture or non-Lambertian surfaces. Implicit view weighting also leaves occlusion reasoning underconstrained.

SynLF addresses these two gaps: PG-LF synthesizes geometry-consistent light fields on the fly from RGB-D, and VisDepth explicitly updates multi-view visibility as it refines disparity.

At inference, the trained model is applied directly to real captures. No target-camera fine-tuning or test-time adaptation is used.

Method

PG-LF supervision and explicit visibility reasoning

PG-LF generates training light fields from 74,013 Hypersim RGB-D images. VisDepth learns from this supervision and uses a frozen monocular prior with iterative visibility-aware matching to estimate disparity.

01 PG-LF synthesis

Scalable supervision from RGB-D

Depth-aware forward splatting preserves occlusion ordering and clean foreground-background transitions. Disparity-coupled defocus and non-Lambertian perturbations model capture effects that corrupt multi-view correspondence.

02 VisDepth

Explicit visibility at every iteration

A frozen Depth Anything V2 (DAv2) prior supplies semantic structure without supplying metric scale. Vis-Proj recomputes per-view visibility from the evolving disparity at every iteration, and visibility-weighted costs drive 24 ConvGRU updates.

Why the synthesis transfers

Real-world benchmark

Calibrated metric depth for a 9×9 light-field camera

Our acquisition system pairs an industrial light-field camera with a structured-light sensor. Checkerboard calibration and a fitted disparity-to-depth mapping align metric ground truth to the LF center view over 0.5–2.5 m.

  • 173 annotated indoor captures
  • 30 outdoor captures for qualitative evaluation
  • Weak texture, specular surfaces, defocus, and semi-transparency

All methods use the same structured-light validity mask for quantitative evaluation. Missing GT, especially on non-Lambertian surfaces, remains excluded.

Current scope

Scope and limitations

Approximate optics. PG-LF models visibility, depth-dependent defocus, and non-Lambertian perturbations, but does not reproduce the full optical image-formation process.

Static scenes. The reported experiments concern static light-field captures. They do not establish performance on dynamic scenes.

Incomplete ground truth. Quantitative evaluation covers valid structured-light pixels. Outdoor captures and regions without GT provide qualitative evidence only.
